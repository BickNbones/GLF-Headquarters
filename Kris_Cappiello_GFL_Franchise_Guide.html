▶MISSION LOG: HOW KRIS CAPPIELLO ENDED UP IN A FANTASY FOOTBALL LEAGUE

CLASSIFICATION: UNCLASSIFIED — ORIGIN DOSSIER

There is a specific type of fantasy football manager that every league has. The guy who joined because someone in the group asked "hey you wanna do fantasy?" and he said yes before he knew what he was agreeing to. Kris Cappiello is that guy, except elevated to an art form. He showed up in 2013, named his team "Space Cadets" — which tells you everything you need to know about how seriously he was taking this — and proceeded to participate in the GFL for eight consecutive years without ever appearing to care deeply about football as a sport.

This is not a criticism. This is a tribute. It takes a special kind of loyalty to show up to something you're indifferent about, every single year, for nearly a decade.

"He didn't join the GFL because he loved fantasy football. He joined because someone asked him. He stayed because... honestly, unclear. But he stayed."

▶DECOMMISSIONED: 2020

END OF MISSION — SPACE CADETS COMMAND RETIRED

After eight years, Kris Cappiello hung up the Space Cadets jersey following the 2020 season. He finished 11th. That's very Kris. He came in 7th, briefly hit 4th twice, spent most of his career in the comfortable numbness of 6th place, and departed with an 11th-place farewell wave. The arc is complete. The story is told.

The GFL without Kris is missing something specific — not a dominant manager, not a championship contender, but something arguably more valuable: a guy who showed up because his friends asked him to, every single year, for eight years straight. He drafted kickers in rounds they had no business being drafted in. He picked Patriots until the Patriots stopped being good enough to pick. He called his team "Space Cadets" from day one, which makes it either brilliant or accidental, and it doesn't matter which.

The GFL is better for having him. The Space Cadets flew their flag for eight years.

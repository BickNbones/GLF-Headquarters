Season 1 — 2021

Year 1: What the F*ck Was That?

Owen Garritt arrived in the GFL in 2021 with the energy of a guy who definitely didn't read the waiver wire instructions. He went 4-10. He scored 1,222 points — which, for context, is fewer points than anyone else in the league that year. He finished 12th out of 12 teams. Dead last. Not "almost last." Not "competitive but unlucky." Last.

The team was called "Prison Purse," which, in hindsight, was an accurate description of how cooked he was. The GFL collectively assumed he was a guy who joined by accident and would quietly disappear.

4-10. 12th place. 1,222 PF. The worst debut in recorded GFL history.

Fun fact: his defense allowed more points (1,448) than most teams score offensively. We don't know how. We didn't ask.

Season 4 — 2024

2024. 11-3. GFL Champion. 🏆

No, we're not joking. We checked three times.

Nobody in the GFL saw 2024 coming. After three consecutive seasons of finishing 9th, 12th, and 10th — after being the punchline, the cautionary tale, the guy the league uses to make other managers feel better about themselves — Owen Garritt went 11-3.

He scored 1,655.96 points, the highest single-season total of his career by a country mile. He had the second-highest PF in the entire league that season (Tyler Shaver had a rough year at 1,447 — Owen cooked him by 200 points). He won the championship. The guy who went dead last in his debut year stood on top of the GFL four years later.

We still don't have a satisfying explanation.

Season 5 — 2025

2025: Back to Being Kinda Mid

The Hangover

Look, not every sequel hits. Owen came back in 2025 as a champion and went 6-8, 8th place, giving up 1,601 points — the most he'd allowed since his rookie disaster. Was it a hangover? Complacency? The football gods laughing at him? Who knows.

The guy scored 1,379 points and gave up 1,601. The math wasn't mathing. The good news: he's been worse. The bad news: he's been way better.

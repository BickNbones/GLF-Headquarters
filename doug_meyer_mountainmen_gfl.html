THE MOUNTAINMEN LORE VAULT

These are the chapters that define the franchise, not a list of transactions or draft picks. Open a card to read the story.

THE FIRST KINGS

Before the GFL had a mythology, Medford supplied its first champion. Doug Meyer took the MountainMen to the top in 2013 and secured something no future franchise can duplicate: being first. The title did more than start a trophy case. It made Medford part of the league's origin story.

THE FALL

The first championship did not become immediate domination. The following seasons sent Medford down the standings and turned the reigning champion into a reminder that elevation is temporary. That drop matters because it made what came next much bigger than a repeat.

RETURN TO GLORY

The 2016 championship changed the meaning of 2013. One title can become a moment. A second title, three seasons later, made the first one impossible to dismiss as an accident. Medford had fallen, climbed again, and reached the summit twice.

THE GHOST OF 2018

The MountainMen went 12–1 in 2018 and finished second after losing the championship to Off Boys. It was a season powerful enough to become a permanent argument: was Medford's greatest team actually the one that did not win a title? The missing ring is exactly what keeps the season alive.

THE LONG CLIMB

Since the second title, the MountainMen have lived with a standard set by their own past. There have been flashes, especially 2018 and a return to winning territory in 2022, but no third championship. Medford's modern story is not collapse. It is the burden of knowing the summit is real because the franchise has already stood there twice.

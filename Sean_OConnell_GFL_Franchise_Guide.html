Chapter I

"The Older Brother Arrives"

Most GFL managers join the league because they love football, hate money, or both. Sean O'Connell joined in 2023 for a third reason: his younger brother Kyle was already there. That's right — Sean didn't found a dynasty from scratch. He walked into a league his little brother had been running in for years, slapped the name "Eskimo Brothers" on his franchise, and got to work.

Year 1 was rough. 5-9, 9th place, gave up 1,551 points. The league noticed — another newcomer, another slow start. Kyle probably said something about it. But Sean had seen enough. He came back in 2024 and proceeded to put up numbers that the GFL had genuinely never seen before.

The older brother entered the younger brother's house. The house was not prepared for what came next.

Sibling Intel

Kyle O'Connell (Off Boys) has been in the GFL since before Sean arrived. The younger brother had a head start. In 2025, Kyle finished 6th. Sean finished 7th. One spot apart. The sibling war is very much alive.

The Centerpiece

2024: The Best Season Nobody Won

The most dominant regular season in the GFL that year — and a playoff exit that still stings.

What He Did

Sean O'Connell put up 1,702.32 points in 2024. That's not just a career high — it's the single-season points record for the entire GFL that year. He went 11-3. His point differential was dominant. By every measurable stat, he was the best team in the league during the regular season. Nobody in the GFL scored more. Nobody.

What Happened

Owen Garritt also went 11-3. Owen scored 1,655.96 points — 46 points fewer than Sean across 14 weeks. And Owen won the championship. Sean lost in the playoffs. The guy with fewer regular season points, the same record, won the ring. Sean went home with a 2nd-place finish and a lot of feelings.
